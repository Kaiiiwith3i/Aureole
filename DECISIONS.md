# Decisions

1. **The registry is authoritative.** An SG2 seal identifies a document version and proves who signed it; the submitted page is still compared against the stored issued PDF. An exact PDF SHA-256 match is the only digital result.
2. **One seal per page.** Page number, page count and content fingerprint are signed. Mixed documents, missing pages and superseded versions get distinct verdicts.
3. **Public reports are redacted.** Anyone can verify, but expected line text and the stored page image require staff sign-in. Issued PDF downloads also require staff sign-in.
4. **Verification stays offline.** RapidOCR and a RandomForest classify local image differences. Gemini and other cloud readers are deferred until real-photo measurements demonstrate a need.
5. **Ambiguous damage is inconclusive.** A known physical mark over a line makes a differing OCR read unreliable. The report does not call that a confirmed text change.
6. **V1 data is preserved.** The old `runtime/registry.db` remains on disk; v2 writes `runtime/registry-v2.db`. The two formats are not interchangeable.
7. **The demo and classifier use issued document pages.** The deleted six-field certificate template is no longer a training or demo dependency. The model's synthetic holdout score is only a smoke check; representative printed photos are still needed for calibration.
8. **QR-only alignment is inconclusive.** A three-photo sweep with all corner markers hidden produced false `MODIFIED` verdicts far from the QR. The seal can still be checked, but comparison requires visible page corners.
9. **Known limits remain visible.** TXT PDFs embed a font per page, and a translucent stamp over text still needs real-print calibration.
10. **A signed scan is an approved copy, not a replacement issue.** Anyone may sign; staff review a complete scan after Signet confirms unchanged printed content. The unsigned original stays valid. Exact approved uploads are recognized by hash; new scans must closely match the approved added-ink mask. This recognizes an approved page appearance, not the signer's identity.
11. **An approval can't upgrade a failed page.** Review found that once a large signature was approved, the same signature plus a short printed amount verified as approved 10 of 10 times: the mark comparison ran regardless of the verdict and tolerated 5% extra ink. The approval is now only considered for a report that is already authentic, and a solid blob of ink present on only one copy (80 px or more) breaks the match. After the fix: added text 10 of 10 `MODIFIED` without approval, a small extra pen mark 10 of 10 without approval, genuine recaptures 10 of 10 still recognised.
12. **A stamp's lettering is not added text.** The added-text rule read "RECEIVED" from the pale fringe of a red stamp and reported `MODIFIED` on 5 of 10 photos. It now reads dark ink only. Printed text added to blank paper is still caught 10 of 10.
13. **A mark covers every line it touches.** A stamp lying across three lines was listed under one of them, so the other two stayed `MISMATCH` (10 of 10 photos `MODIFIED`). The covered-line rule now applies to each line the mark's pixels reach: those photos are `INCONCLUSIVE`. Trade-off unchanged from v1: an edit hidden under a stamp is inconclusive, never authentic.
14. **Classifier trained on varied scenes.** 40 scenes per kind over three page shapes, with mark size and position varied on lines and on blank paper (was 12 scenes of one fixed size on one page; a stamp in a blank area scored 0.43 and produced no note). About 2 minutes on a MacBook Air M4; synthetic holdout 0.98, which is a smoke check and not field accuracy.
15. **A mark covers the lines inside its outline.** Independent QA: 8 of 132 stamps and stains still gave `MODIFIED`, because a ring garbles a neighbouring line (read confidently as "EMPLCYMENT") without putting its own ink on it. Covering now uses the mark's convex hull plus the OCR crop margin.
16. **Codes are content.** QA pasted a payment QR onto a genuine page and got `AUTHENTIC` 6 of 6. Every QR or 2D barcode other than the seal is now decoded on both the scan and the issued page; one that isn't on the issued page is `MODIFIED`. This also catches a document's own payment code being swapped.
17. **Nothing big goes unmentioned, and nothing critical is ignored.** A 600 x 100 px black bar on blank paper was `AUTHENTIC` with an "unknown" finding, and a finding could be labelled critical while the verdict said no differences. A large unexplained added mark inside the content box is now a note, and a classification below 0.6 is shown as unknown. The 1500 px threshold rests on simulated photos, whose noise stays at the page edges.
