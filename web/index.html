How it works

One spell, four steps.

No thousand-year training arc required. Staff seal a document once; after that anyone can check a copy of it.

1Seal

Staff upload a TXT, DOCX, PDF or XLS file. Aureole turns it into a PDF and frames every page with four corner markers, a signed QR seal and a printed fingerprint.

2Record

The registry keeps the issued file, its fingerprint and every printed line. A document is legitimate only if this registry has it. Think of it as a grimoire where every entry is one you actually use.

3Check

Submit the file, or a photo of the printed pages. An identical file is an exact match. A photo is flattened using the corner markers, then every printed line is read and compared with the registry's copy.

4Explain

Aureole boxes each difference and says what kind it is: a changed line, added text, or just a stamp, fold or stain. It reports what it sees. It never says who did it, or why.

A chest can look like treasure. Check what is inside.

What it catches

Treasure, or mimic?

A chest can look exactly right and still bite. Aureole never trusts a seal on its own: it opens the page and compares it with the registry's copy. The registry is where the real answer comes from.

  • One changed digitEvery printed line is read back and compared, so 48,500 becoming 98,500 is flagged.
  • Text added to blank paperThe whole page is protected, not just the lines that were there.
  • A swapped or added QR codePayment codes and other 2D codes on the page are compared with the issued ones.
  • A seal copied onto another documentThe seal points at one specific page. Different content under it is the mimic.
  • An old or withdrawn versionReissued and revoked documents still carry a genuine seal. The registry knows they are no longer current.
  • Stamps, folds and stains, as notesWear and office stamps are listed, not treated as tampering. Damage alone is never called a change.

Verdicts

Seven answers, in plain words.

The look is fantasy. The results are not: every report uses one of these, with the exact place it applies.

  • AUTHENTICNothing differs from what was issued. For the issued file itself this is an exact, byte-for-byte match.
  • AUTHENTIC, WITH NOTESThe printed text matches. Something else is on the page, such as a stamp, a fold or a signature.
  • MODIFIEDA line reads differently, or text or a code was added. The report shows where.
  • INCONCLUSIVEPart of the page couldn't be read, or a page is missing. Try a sharper, full-page photo.
  • REVOKEDThe seal is genuine, but the issuer has withdrawn or replaced this version.
  • INVALID SEALThe seal wasn't signed by a trusted issuer.
  • NOT ISSUEDThere is no Aureole seal, or the registry has no matching record. The registry decides.

Signed after issue

Staff can review and approve a signed copy. The unsigned original stays valid, and an approval never covers anything added later.

The original stays private

Anyone can verify and see where a page differs. Only signed-in staff see the issued text and the original page.

Runs on your machine

Text reading and change classification both run locally. Nothing is sent to a cloud service, and it works with the Wi-Fi off.

What Aureole can't promise yet
  • Accuracy so far is measured on simulated photos, not on real prints.
  • DOCX and XLS files need LibreOffice on the issuing machine. PDF and TXT don't.
  • A seal proves what a page says. It can't tell which sheet of paper is the original.
